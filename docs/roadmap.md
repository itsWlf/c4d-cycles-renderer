# Status and roadmap

What works, what is approximate, what is missing, and what is planned. This is the project's checklist: tick a box when the work is done and verified, and move anything newly found into the right list. How things work is in [architecture.md](architecture.md); how to work on them is in [development.md](development.md).

Words used here: **verified** means a test compares the feature with Cinema 4D's Standard renderer (or with Redshift, or measures it on its own where neither can serve) and passes. **Untested** means the code exists and has not been run for that case.

## Where the project stands

- Final frames and the IPR work end to end on Windows with Cinema 4D 2024, 2026 and 2027 (measured with 2024.5.1, 2026.3.4 and 2027.0.0; supported since 0.2.0, before only 2027) and Blender 5.0, 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3 (measured with 5.0.0, 5.1.2, 5.2.3 LTS and 5.3.0; since 0.2.0, before only 5.2), for Cycles and Eevee. Cinema 4D 2025 is supported too, but its installation here has no Python, so only its loading, a scene with the plugin's tags and lights open in it, and final frames through `Commandline.exe` were seen ([reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Cinema 4D versions").
- The test baseline is 63 tests (13 for the bridge alone, 50 inside Cinema 4D; `redshift_lookalikes` added for F19's look-alikes, `ipr_reuse` and `ipr_follow_window` added for F15 and F16 of 0.2.0, `test_versions` for F8, `ipr_lights` and `ipr_lights_window` for F18, `ipr_navigate` and `ipr_navigate_window` for F17). It runs on Cinema 4D 2027 with Blender 5.2 once per sprint; before a major release also on each other Cinema 4D version with Blender 5.2 (`-C4D`) and on each other Blender with Cinema 4D 2027 (`-Blender`; the maintainer's rule of 2026-11-16, `docs/development.md`, "Which versions, when"). On 2026-11-16 (F9, Blender 5.2.3, the two tests of the Cycles Native Material on hold and not counted): Cinema 4D 2027.0.0 56 of 56 (1143 s), 2026.3.4 56 of 56 (1075 s), 2024.5.1 56 of 56 (1078 s), no Blender left running; 2025 cannot run the tests here (no Python in its installation). On 2026-11-16 (F8, the two tests of the Cycles Native Material on hold and not counted): 5.0.0 and 5.1.2 55 of 56 (`volumes`, explained and handled since, then passed alone), 5.2.3 56 of 56, 5.3.0 54 of 56 (`test_viewport`, fixed since and then passed alone; `camera_tag`'s IPR panorama, open: "Open bugs"); no Blender left running. 15 to 18 minutes per version. The 44 of then all passed for 0.1.0, and again on 2026-11-12 after the 0.2.0 bug fixes; the two camera tests added since (`test_cameras`, `camera_tag`), the extended `cameras`, the four tests of F4 to F13 (`test_output`, `output`, `image_nodes`, `ipr_add`), the extended `passes`, and the five tests of F10, F11, F12 and F14 (`test_lights`, `cycles_lights`, `redshift_nodes`, `test_devices`, `devices`; `ipr_add` and `native_material` extended) passed in the selected runs of their work, the whole baseline has not run with them yet; both with one start of Cinema 4D per test and with the tests sharing starts (`docs/development.md`, "Tests"). For each piece of work only the tests for what changed are run (`tools/run_tests.ps1 -Changed`); the whole baseline runs once per sprint, before a commit the maintainer asks for, and before a release.
- Version 0.1.0 is published as the first public release (branch `0.1.0`, tag `v0.1.0`). Work for 0.2.0 is on `main`.

## Open bugs

- [ ] **Cycles Native Material: what is edited in Blender's Shader Editor does not reliably arrive in Cinema 4D, so the material is switched off in 0.1.0.** Reported twice from use by hand: first that nodes of the Texture category "and some others" are not synced once they are connected, then, after the fixes below, that nodes and their values still go missing. The cause is not known.
  - **Switched off:** without `g_cyclesRenderNativeMaterial=true` there is no command, and the material type is registered but hidden, so a scene that holds such a material still loads and renders. The code is all there.
  - **Not reproduced by any test.** Every node of the table, wired in from Python, is saved; a Checker wired with simulated mouse events is too; textures arrive in the final render and in the IPR. Not covered: a real hand at the mouse and keyboard, adding nodes from Blender's Add menu, choosing an image in the file browser, changing values with sliders.
  - **Found and fixed on the way** (see "Fixed for 0.1.0"): Ctrl+Z in the editor window and any error in the editor's timer each ended all syncing for the rest of the session. They did not settle it.
  - **Where to go on:** reproduce it by hand with the console of the editor's Blender open (*Window > Toggle System Console*): a failure in the editor's timer is printed there (`_tick` in `bridge/c4d_bridge/scene/native.py`). Compare what the editor shows with the `.blend` and `.state` files in the edit folder, to tell a save that did not happen from one Cinema 4D did not take in. Places not yet ruled out, from reading the code: `_load` takes the first material of a file; in the IPR's Blender the old material is swapped for the new one while the viewport renders.
- [x] **Undoing "Camera with Cycles Camera Tag" (the IPR's Add menu) left the render view without its camera** (found 2026-11-12 with F11, fixed 2026-11-13, not released). Cause, measured in `c4dpy` and in the application: Cinema 4D's undo cannot hold a view. After undoing any step that recorded the render view with `AddUndo` (`CHANGE`, `CHANGE_NOCHILDREN` or `CHANGE_SMALL`, the camera set with `SetSceneCamera` or as `BASEDRAW_DATA_CAMERA`), the document's view has no camera link at all and looks through its own editor camera (the "RS Camera", type 1057516, that is no object of the document); redo does not bring the link back; the view object held before the undo is no longer the document's (which is why the old check, reading that stale object, passed). Fix: the view stays out of the undo step; the plugin follows the step itself (`FollowAddedCameras` in `ipr/ipr_add.cpp`, called by the renderer's message plugin on every change event and once a second): while the added camera is gone the view looks through the camera it had before, once it is back (redo) through the added camera again; a switch is forgotten when its document closes or the user chooses another camera for that view. Tested: `ipr_add.py` (`camera_undone`, `camera_redone`, `camera_undone_again`; they fail on the old build), and with Cinema 4D's own Undo and Redo commands in the application (`ipr_window_checks.py`, `add_camera_undo_redo`). Side effect, by design: deleting the added camera by hand also puts the view back on the earlier camera (Cinema 4D itself would fall back to the editor camera).
- [x] **A scene of more than about 800 meshes could not be sent to Blender at all** (found 2026-11-13 with F16, fixed, not released). Every mesh is five blobs, the protocol allowed 4096 per frame, and every request carried an empty blob for each blob of an unchanged item it left out, so the first request and every later one of a 900-mesh scene (4500 blobs) made the bridge end the connection ("The connection to Blender was lost while sending"); final frames go through the same code. Now a request carries only the blobs of the items in it, and both sides accept 2^20 blobs (`protocol/protocol.md`, "Frames"). A bridge of 0.1.0 still refuses more than 4096. Tested: `test_protocol` (5000 blobs echoed in both modes), `ipr_reuse` (a 900-mesh scene in the IPR).
- [ ] **The IPR can miss that the active document changed, when the document it showed was the only one and is closed.** Found while making the tests run several to one start of Cinema 4D (2026-11-12): `InsertBaseDocument` replaces an untouched "Untitled" document, so closing the inserted one leaves none and Cinema 4D makes a new empty one. In `c4dpy` after `tests/c4d/redshift_material.py` the IPR then never sent the new document (nothing sent in 8 s), kept the closed one's scene in Blender, and so kept its baked files; in a fresh `c4dpy` the same steps worked. With another document in the list the IPR switches every time. Likely cause, not verified: `IprController::Tick` tells documents apart by their address (`doc != _document`), and the new document can be made at the address of the one just freed. Not seen by hand yet. `tests/c4d/baked_files.py` now keeps a second document in the list, so it tests the switch it means to; a test of this case itself is open.
- [x] **Colour management set to sRGB (legacy) gave wrong results** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). The render space "Legacy (sRGB linear workflow)" is Cinema 4D's "Basic" colour management. There, colours typed into Cinema 4D (materials, shaders, lights, the sky) rendered too bright with Linear Workflow on, because they were not linearised as Cinema 4D does; with Linear Workflow off every texture rendered too dark; with Multi-Pass on the picture got the sRGB curve twice; the IPR window applied the curve also without Linear Workflow; and the IPR's snapshot showed linear values without any curve in the Picture Viewer, too dark. The likely cause of the report: a texture without Linear Workflow, or a snapshot. Now every colour source, the buffer, the Picture Viewer, the IPR window, its snapshot and its saved EXR match the Standard renderer in every combination of Linear Workflow and input colour profile (`tests/c4d/colour.py`; numbers in the SDK reference, "Colour management"). What the dialog's legacy entry does to Linear Workflow and to the colours was not found out (its menu ignores posted input), so all combinations are covered. Not measured in that mode, and converted by analogy: clone colours, the hair gradient, the Cycles Object tag's volume colours, the world colour; see "Untested".
- [x] **Cinema 4D's fixed camera projections and the editor's parallel views rendered wrong** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). Every projection but Perspective and Parallel (Left, Right, Front, Back, Top, Bottom, Military, Frog, Bird, Gentleman, Isometric, Dimetric) was sent as a parallel camera turned like the camera object, while Cinema 4D ignores the camera's rotation for these and looks along the projection's own axes; the editor's views of those projections (no camera object) therefore showed a front view; the four oblique ones had no slant; a parallel camera clipped what lay behind its plane, which Cinema 4D shows; Cinema 4D's spherical camera rendered as a perspective one. All of it, final frames and the IPR, now matches the Standard renderer (`tests/c4d/cameras.py`, `camera_tag.py`; measurements in the SDK reference, "Cameras and views").
- [x] **Cycles nodes: the three curve nodes had no control for their curves** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). RGB Curves (C, R, G, B), Vector Curves (X, Y, Z) and Float Curve now have one Cinema 4D spline control per curve in the Attribute Manager, and the setting "Extend" (Blender's Extrapolated or Horizontal). A new node starts with a straight line and renders exactly as Blender's own new node. The plugin evaluates each spline as Cinema 4D draws it and sends it as points joined by straight lines; Blender's curve follows it within 0.0001 of its height (measured on S curves, hand-set tangents, cubic knots and overshooting Bezier segments), corners land exactly. Limits: see "Known limits", "Cycles nodes, curves". Tested by `tests/c4d/cycles_nodes.py` (a curve set as a spline renders as Cinema 4D evaluates it, for each node) and `tests/bridge/test_scene.py` (Cycles and Eevee).

### Fixed for 0.1.0

- [x] **Ctrl+Z in the Shader Editor of a native material ended its syncing.** Blender's undo reads the changed datablocks anew, so the bridge lost the material; the first undo even went back to before the material existed. The bridge now pushes an undo step when the editor opens, finds its datablocks again after every undo and redo, saves the undone state, and steps forward again when an undo goes back beyond the opening of the editor.
- [x] **A preview picture of a material that shows nothing** (a Transparent BSDF, a Holdout) was never taken, so Cinema 4D kept the old thumbnail.
- [x] **Cycles nodes without a control for their value:** Value, RGB and Normal have their value as a setting in the Attribute Manager; the Color Ramp has its interpolation and up to eight stops (position, colour, alpha) there.
- [x] **Editor windows of native materials stayed open** when their material was deleted or its document closed. The plugin now closes the window.

- [ ] **Eevee shows some objects with another object's per-object number** (found 2026-11-17 with F19's look-alikes, not fixed). A new scene of 256 planes, each with the Jitter look-alike (an Attribute node reading the object's custom property `cr_name_key`), rendered as a final frame by Cycles and then by Eevee: in Eevee 12 of the 256 objects show the value of another object, the same 12 every time (Blender 5.2.3 and 5.3.0; none with 5.0.0); Cycles is right, and so is Eevee when it renders that scene first, and in every later scene of the same run (`tests/c4d/redshift_lookalikes.py` reports the count as `random/float_eevee_after_cycles`). Each render starts a Blender of its own, and the two scene requests are the same (their headers and blob sizes captured with `g_cyclesRenderCapture`: identical), so the cause lies on Blender's side; Attribute types Object and Instancer behave alike. The cause is not known. Next: reproduce in Blender alone with the bridge's test client (`tests/bridge/test_geometry.py`, `check_numbers`, with 256 objects) and look at Eevee's object attribute buffers.
- [x] **The temporary scene hook id is replaced.** The IPR's scene hook (`ipr/ipr_scenehook.cpp`) was built with the test number 1000001 for one day; it now has its issued id, 1074340 (`ids.h`, `ID_IPR_SCENE_HOOK`). The test number was never committed or published; only scenes saved on the developer's machine with the build of 2026-11-16 carry it.
- [ ] **IPR with an equirectangular Cycles Camera tag on Blender 5.3 shows the panorama shrunk** (found 2026-11-16 with F8, not fixed). `tests/c4d/camera_tag.py`, `ipr_equirectangular`: at 720 x 360 the IPR's picture holds the panorama about 280 x 75 pixels large in its lower left, in three runs on 5.3.0 (the baseline, and alone twice); on 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 it is right, final frames are right on 5.3, and the same check after a resize of the IPR window (`ipr_equirectangular_fit_window`) passes on 5.3. Not reproduced through the bridge alone (`view_start` with a panoramic camera, after a resize, after a switch from a perspective camera, after the free view: framed alike on 5.2 and 5.3). Next: run `camera_tag.py` on 5.3 with Blender's window shown (*Options > Show Blender Window*, or `view_window`) and compare the region's camera-frame settings with 5.2's at the failing frame.

## Known faults in translation

Each was measured and set aside. The test of each reports the number without failing.

- [ ] **Filter shader bakes too dark.** Hue is right, brightness is not (difference 0.31). Try Hue/Saturation, Brightness/Contrast and Gamma nodes instead of baking, or find the colour space the bake works in.
- [ ] **Shaders that work in 3D space are baked over the UV square.** The default Noise ("Texture" space), 3D gradients and the surface shaders show the right kind of pattern in the wrong place. Needs node versions of Cinema 4D's noises or a bake per object in its own space.
- [ ] **A bitmap's OCIO input colour space is not read.** Files are sent as sRGB, or linear for .exr and .hdr, whatever the shader says. Seen while fixing the legacy mode: an EXR written by Cinema 4D's `BaseBitmap.Save` without a colour profile renders in the Standard renderer as if it were sRGB (0.2 becomes 0.033), in Cycles Render as linear (0.2). EXRs of other programs were not tried. (With "Basic" colour management the bitmap's "Color Profile" is read.)
- [ ] **Cubic projection is mirrored or turned on some sides** (94.7 % agreement). It uses Blender's box projection of images; the fix is to build the six sides from nodes, and to apply the tag's offset there too.
- [ ] **Clone colours for MoGraph generators other than the Cloner.** Fracture, MoText, Matrix and the rest render without them. The Color shader's other modes and the Multi shader are not translated.
- [x] **Baked textures are removed, and reused** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). Each Cinema 4D keeps them in a folder of its own, `<preferences>/cyclesrender/baked/session-<process id>-<time>`; final renders and the IPR reuse a file while nothing it depends on changed (an unchanged shader is not baked again at another frame unless it changes over time by itself); a file is deleted once every document it was made for has been closed and no Blender reads it, and everything goes when Cinema 4D quits. What a crashed session left, and what 0.1.0 left directly in `baked`, is removed at the next start. Left as it is: a document that was never in the document list (made by a script, rendered by the Render Queue or `Commandline.exe`) keeps its files until Cinema 4D quits. Tested by `baked_files` and `baked_files_window`.
- [ ] **Eevee does not blur a deforming mesh.** It ignores the `velocity` attribute; it needs the mesh itself to change over the shutter.
- [ ] **Instancers and lights have no motion blur.** The copies of a Cloner or multi-instance stand still during the shutter.
- [ ] **Pyro has never rendered real smoke in a test.** The code path is the one a Volume Builder uses, but a scripted simulation stays empty. Try a Pyro scene by hand, then check the density scale and the fire's temperature against Redshift.
- [ ] **Hair material:** only the colour gradient and the thickness are read.
- [ ] **Redshift sheen is fainter** than in Redshift (difference 0.18).
- [ ] **Volumes made in Cinema 4D render empty in the Cycles of Blender 5.0 and 5.1**: Cinema 4D 2027 writes VDB format 225 (Volume Builder, Pyro), which their Cycles renders empty (Eevee shows it; files of format 224 of other programs render); the console says so. Use Blender 5.2 or newer for them. A way around would be writing format 224, if the SDK allows it [untried].
- [ ] **Redshift subsurface with Blender 5.0 and 5.1** differs more from Redshift (0.164 on 5.0.0, tolerance 0.15 on 5.2): Blender 5.2 changed how Random Walk maps the colour, and the older versions have only the old mapping. The test allows 0.2 before 5.2.
- [ ] **Redshift transmission depth, dispersion and thin-walled** are not translated; depth could become a Volume Absorption node.
- [ ] **OpenPBR's coat darkening** (on by default) darkens the base under a coat in Redshift, which Blender's coat does not: a dark blue coated ball renders 0.14 brighter (`redshift_nodes`, `openpbr_coat`, reported without failing; named in the console).
- [ ] **Redshift's AO node** samples a cone (spread, falloff) that Blender's Ambient Occlusion node lacks: a corner renders 0.075 darker; spread and falloff are named in the console.

## Planned features

- [ ] **macOS.** What a port needs is listed in the development guide. Nothing has been run on a Mac.
- [ ] **Team Render.** Nothing is built or tested. Each client would render whole frames with its own plugin, bridge and Blender; open questions are which files travel with the scene (textures of node materials, .vdb files, a dome light's map) and how to test with a second machine.
- [x] **Skip the export of unchanged meshes in the IPR** (built in 0.2.0, not released; Preferences > Renderer > Cycles Render > IPR, "Skip Unchanged Meshes", on by default). A mesh Blender holds is not exported again when nothing it is made from changed: for a polygon object of the document its identity (address, GUID, the dirty counts of object and tags) decides, for everything a generator, deformer or instance makes a hash of all the mesh's data (`scene/scene_exporter.cpp`, `MeshSignature`). The scene walk, objects, materials, lights and the world are still written every time and compared as before. In a scene of 900 meshes (720,000 polygons) the export after moving one object went from 74–87 ms to 8–15 ms, the change to the picture from 120–131 ms to 50–63 ms (most of what is left is Cycles restarting with 900 objects). 33 kinds of change were checked against a full export (`tests/c4d/ipr_reuse.py`; list in the SDK reference, "Skipping unchanged meshes in the IPR").
- [ ] **A region drawn in the IPR window itself**, and the plugin's picture drawn live into Cinema 4D's own viewport.
- [ ] **Keep one Blender for Cinema 4D's Interactive Render Region.** Each refresh is a render job of its own today and starts its own Blender (about 3 s).
- [ ] **More render passes:** Cryptomatte (it needs a manifest that Cinema 4D's multi-pass layers have no place for: the maintainer to say where the passes are composited), light groups (the Cycles Light object and tag exist now and could carry Blender's light group; not built, as the passes' place is open), custom AOVs (they need AOV Output nodes in the Cycles node space). Done in 0.2.0: motion vectors, object and material index, shadow catcher, volume direct and indirect, sample count, Eevee's shadow and transparent.
- [ ] **More shaders as nodes instead of baked:** Tiles and Brick, circular and diagonal gradients, further blend modes of Fusion.
- [ ] **More of Redshift:** the 47 nodes not yet translated (`internal/redshift-nodes.md`, section 3, each with its estimate: Noise and Maxon Noise, Tiles, the Car Paint, Skin, SSS, Architectural and older materials, Distorter, hair, volume and environment nodes, displacement, the Random Material Switch; 0.2.0 translates 112 of 159, F10 and F19, four of them as look-alikes: Jitter, Random Color Switch, Brick, Flakes), IES and sun lights, the remaining area shapes, the Redshift Object tag, the camera's depth of field and its own projections (the Redshift camera's panoramas are not read; it is read like Cinema 4D's camera).
- [ ] **The preview and the viewport look of a Cycles Native Material** before its first edit and in Cinema 4D's viewport (grey today).
- [ ] **The window's texts in the string resources** (English strings in the code today).
- [ ] **Pause the IPR while a Picture Viewer render runs.**
- [ ] **Redshift as the baseline.** Compare with Redshift instead of Cinema 4D's Standard renderer, in the tests and as the model for what is translated: Redshift is closer to Cycles in what it can do (bounced light, depth of field, physically based materials and lights).
- [ ] **An image dropped on the Node Editor makes an Image Texture node** that names the file. Built in 0.2.0 (the node space names the node and its ports); not yet tried with a real drop by hand, from the file browser or the Asset Browser.
- [ ] **An object dropped on the Node Editor makes the node that refers to it** (for example Object Info or a texture coordinate from the object).
- [ ] **A more responsive IPR:** the picture follows the camera while it is being moved, and objects while they are being moved, instead of updating when the movement ends. *Partly built in 0.2.0:* during a tool's drag (an object or a camera moved with the Move, Rotate or Scale tool) the IPR sends every step to Blender (Cinema 4D's tools announce each step with `EVMSG_ASYNCEDITORMOVE`; the window's timer does not run during a drag), and it follows playback frame by frame (before: nothing until playback stopped). Measured in the application with an emulated drag and Cinema 4D's own playback (`tests/c4d/ipr_follow_window.py`). *Open:* whether the IPR window draws the new pictures during a real drag (in the emulated drag nothing processes Cinema 4D's events, so they show when it ends), and whether navigating a view (Alt and the mouse) sends anything during the drag at all; both need a hand (`tests/c4d/show_ipr_trace.py` says what to try). If navigation sends nothing, following it needs a scene hook (one exists since F18, with a temporary id). *Built in 0.2.0 (F18): light values.* A light's value dragged in the Attribute Manager (intensity, colour, cone, size, the Cycles Light's power, the Cycles Light tag's values) is sent at every step of the drag, the light alone, and the picture is drawn during the drag (before: nothing until the mouse was released). Measured with mouse messages posted to the Attribute Manager's sliders: 39 to 40 of about 40 steps sent, writing the lights 0.02–0.09 ms, a new frame 3 ms after each, on screen 12–21 ms after each (`docs/architecture.md`, "The IPR"). Cinema 4D's, Redshift's and the Cycles Lights; lights made by generators follow when the drag ends. *Open:* a real hand at the Attribute Manager, a number field's arrows, the colour picker window, and a light's handles in the view (emulated only).
- [x] **Navigating the scene from the IPR window** (built in 0.2.0, not released; Preferences > Renderer > Cycles Render > IPR, "Navigate in the IPR Window", on by default): a drag in the IPR's picture with Cinema 4D's navigation keys orbits, pans or dollies the camera the IPR renders through, and the picture follows during the drag. Alt with the left, middle or right button, or the keys of Cinema 4D's key map for Rotate, Move and Scale Camera (by default 3, 1, 2) held while dragging; plain drags and the wheel still pan and zoom the picture (`NavigationForDrag` in `ipr/ipr_navigation.cpp`, the one place the scheme is decided). The camera moved is a camera object of the document (one undo step per drag; refused, with a line in the status bar, for a Protection tag or a locked layer) or the editor camera of the view the IPR renders through (that view's own "Undo View"); Escape puts it back. Orbit turns around the point under the mouse (found against the meshes that render) or the point of interest, pan keeps that point under the mouse, dolly heads for it; a parallel view zooms (Cinema 4D's `CAMERA_ZOOM`, or the Cycles Camera tag's orthographic scale), a fixed projection (Top, Front, ...) pans and zooms but is not orbited; Cinema 4D's navigation preferences are followed (point of interest, cursor placement, dolly to cursor, reverse orbit, trackball and Ctrl's trackball, the three speeds). Not while the IPR is stopped or paused, nor with its camera locked. Measured with mouse messages posted to the picture: every step of a drag sent, the picture on screen 4 to 6 ms after a step (median), Cinema 4D's view redrawn at each step (`docs/architecture.md`, "The IPR"). *Open, by hand:* Alt with the mouse buttons (Alt cannot be posted), Ctrl's trackball, how speeds and directions feel against a view, keys held over the window.
- [x] **More Blender versions** (built in 0.2.0, not released): Blender 5.0, 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3 with one plugin and one bridge. What differs between them is handled in the bridge (5.0 has no `PointCloud.resize`) and named by the plugin where the user should know it (Eevee before 5.2 shows no light to the camera; Eevee renders panoramas from 5.3 on, so the warning about them is gone there); the list is in the Blender reference, "Version coupling". The node table is made from all four (`tools/make_nodes.py`, which fails on a node a new version brings that no table names): every node, socket, setting and option knows the version it exists from (generated list: [reference/cycles-nodes.md](reference/cycles-nodes.md)), and what the Blender in use lacks is left out with a line in the console; the four Eevee-only material lighting nodes of 5.3 are in the node space. A node the chosen Blender lacks shows a red card on itself ("Unsupported in Blender 5.2.3"), a node not every supported version has a grey one, and every such node has the comment "Needs Blender 5.3 or later" under it (both agreed with the maintainer). Render settings an older version lacks are not sent to it. Without a Blender chosen in the Preferences, detection picks the one the bridge is installed into, else 5.2 (the long-term support release, while 5.3 has open bugs), else the newest. `tools/run_tests.ps1 -Blender <version>`; the baseline ran once per version for F8, and from now on runs on the other versions before a major release (5.2 otherwise). Adding a version: development guide, "Generated files".
- [x] **More Cinema 4D versions** (built in 0.2.0, not released): Cinema 4D 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 from one source tree, each built against its own SDK (`tools/build_plugin.ps1 -Version <year>|all`); what differs between the SDKs is answered in `source/sdk_compat.h` (list in the SDK reference, "Cinema 4D versions"). **One package for all four, nothing to delete:** the folder `cyclesrender` holds `cyclesrender_2024.xdl64` to `cyclesrender_2027.xdl64` and one `res`, and each Cinema 4D loads the build of its own version and passes over the others without a dialog or a console line (`Commandline.exe` names them in its output as warnings, which the install notes call expected; measured in all four; Cinema 4D's own module loader decides by the ABI and core framework version a module carries, so a loader module would neither work nor be needed). Differences of Cinema 4D's own behaviour that the plugin follows (found by running the baseline in 2024 and 2026; list with measurements in the SDK reference): before 2026.2 the render document is prepared for Cinema 4D's renderer, which hid "Render Perfect" spheres (fixed); 2024 renders an object without material brighter in OCIO documents (the plugin's default grey follows); 2024 could not bake a shader with a bitmap inside in the render document (the plugin bakes a copy in a document of its own), gives links new identities in every render's copy (the bake cache leaves them out of its fingerprint), and writes Volume Builder grids without a name (the plugin names them and writes the file itself). `tools/run_tests.ps1 -C4D <year>`; the baseline ran once per version for F9, and from now on runs on 2024, 2025 and 2026 before a major release (2027 otherwise; results: "Where the project stands"). **Cinema 4D 2025 could not be tested here**: its installation on the development machine has no Python runtime, so neither c4dpy nor the test harness runs; its build loads and was checked without Python as far as that goes (see "Untested").
- [x] **Blender's device settings in the Cycles Render preferences** (built in 0.2.0, not released): Preferences > Renderer > Cycles Render > Cycles Render Devices: a device type (Automatic, None, OptiX, CUDA, HIP, oneAPI, Metal) and a checkbox per device of that type and the CPU, as in Blender's own Preferences; "Find Devices" asks a Blender for the list. They decide what the Render Settings' device "GPU" uses, in final frames and the IPR; Blender's own preferences are not touched. Rendered on OptiX and CUDA (this machine); HIP, oneAPI and Metal untested (only their fallback).

## Known limits

- **The IPR needs a desktop session** and renders into a hidden Blender window. Its resolution can exceed the desktop's (3840 × 2160 tested), but every frame is read back on Blender's main thread: about 1 ms at 960 × 540 and 20 ms at 3840 × 2160.
- **The IPR shows no motion blur** (Cycles' viewport has none), and it uses Blender's viewport settings, so small differences from the final render are possible.
- **On the CPU a 1920 × 1080 IPR is slow to react** (0.8 s for an edit). Use the GPU or a smaller IPR resolution.
- **The IPR during a drag:** an object or camera moved with a tool is sent to Blender at every step of the drag, but whether the window can draw the new pictures before the drag ends depends on Cinema 4D's drag loop and was not seen (no test can make a real drag). While a value is dragged in the Attribute Manager only the lights follow (the lights alone, with the include and exclude lists of the last full export); any other value dragged there (a material, a generator, a camera's focal length) and the lights a generator makes follow when the mouse is released. Navigating a view (Alt and the mouse) is followed when the drag ends, unless Cinema 4D sends a message during it (unknown); navigating in the IPR's own picture is followed during the drag (since 0.2.0). With "Skip Unchanged Meshes" the walk, objects and materials are still exported after every change: a scene of tens of thousands of objects or many complex materials still costs time per change; hair, volumes and particles are always exported in full.
- **Navigating from the IPR's picture:** the orbit's and dolly's rates per pixel (0.4° and e^-0.005 at 100 % speed) and the dolly's direction (right and up bring the camera nearer) are estimates, to be compared with a view by hand (Cinema 4D's own rates could not be measured: posted input does not drive a view); all are constants at the top of `ipr/ipr_navigation.cpp`. The wheel zooms the picture and does not dolly. The point under the mouse is found on meshes only (not hair, volumes, particles) and not through a panoramic lens (a panorama orbits its point of interest and pans by an approximate scale). What Cinema 4D's view keeps as its point of interest is not readable: the IPR uses the camera's target distance, or where its own last drag left the point. Cinema 4D's Ctrl "view snapping", "Deep" and "Sync Orthographic Views" are not followed. A camera driven by animation stays where the drag left it until the time changes, as in Cinema 4D's view (no keys are set). Cinema 4D's status bar says what is moved, or why nothing is; its managers (the Attribute Manager's coordinates) update when the drag ends.
- **Lights without falloff** cannot be reproduced by a physically based renderer; they match Cinema 4D at one distance. Cinema 4D's area lights shine to both sides, Blender's to one.
- **Reflectance** is reduced to one specular lobe and one clear coat; the Principled BSDF has no layer stack.
- **Displacement** moves only the points a mesh has (no sub-polygon displacement).
- **A Sphere primitive with "Render Perfect"** shows its polygons; Cinema 4D's own renderers draw it perfectly round.
- **Clones that all differ** become one instancer each, which is slow from a few thousand on.
- **Two texture tags on one object that use different UVW tags:** the mesh has one render UV set, that of the tag covering the whole object.
- **Render passes** are written as rendered; the view transform is never baked into them. With "Basic" colour management that is what Cinema 4D's own layers hold too. Eevee has no UV, indirect, transmission, volume indirect, object or material index, sample count or shadow catcher pass; Cycles has no Shadow and no Transparent pass (measured with Blender 5.2.3; the Render Settings hide each for the engine that lacks it, and a pass asked for anyway is named in the console). The Vector pass is Cycles's and Eevee's motion to the frame before and after, in pixels; Cycles gives none with motion blur on. The Object Index is the first enabled Object Buffer of a Compositing tag (further ones are named in the console); the Material Index is the "Pass Index" of a Cycles node material's Material Output (classic materials: 0). Cinema 4D's own Object Buffer layers (one mask per id) are not made.
- **Pixel aspect:** the IPR renders square pixels and shows the picture as wide as the pixel aspect makes it (400 × 200 pixels of aspect 2 are 800 × 200 in the IPR at "100 %"). The Output tab's edits are not part of Cinema 4D's undo for the Output page (undo restores the tab's own values, which then show Cinema 4D's again), and Cinema 4D's Film Aspect field is not updated by them (it was not when Python set the pixel aspect either; Cinema 4D's picture follows Width, Height and Pixel Aspect).
- **Image nodes:** a path made relative is relative to the folder of the active document (the buttons work on the material the Node Editor shows); an image sequence is not translated (its file is used as one image, with a note in the console); a file of the Asset Browser is copied into the session's folder of texture files [untested].
- **Render settings without effect:** the four Subdivisions values act on Blender's adaptive subdivision, which the plugin does not use (it sends the mesh Cinema 4D subdivided).
- **Cycles Light object:** it does not light Cinema 4D's viewport or Cinema 4D's own renderers, and has no green "enabled" check in the Object Manager (it is switched off with its render dot or a layer). Its light node tree and light groups are not there. Its viewport drawing shows type, size and cone at a fixed display size; it has no handles.
- **Cycles Light tag:** Blender's radius, soft falloff, sun angle and spread come only with the Light tab, which replaces all of the light's emission; with the tab off the light renders as before. A light keeps its own "Shadow" setting until the Settings tab's Cast Shadow decides. The tag counts on the light it sits on only.
- **Render devices:** the device list is what a Blender reported when "Find Devices" was pressed (a new card needs another press); the material thumbnails still render on the CPU. Several GPUs of one type were not tried.
- **Redshift's look-alikes** (Jitter, Random Color Switch, Brick, Flakes): each object gets another random value than in Redshift (the same ranges and shares, varying with the same objects: per path of names, per Redshift Object ID, per User Data number; one value for the copies of a render instance or multi-instance, as in Redshift); Brick's and Flakes' noises and cells are Blender's (Brick's layout, gap widths, colours and spreads, Flakes' size, share and tilt measured to agree); Flakes do not fade with distance (Redshift fades by the pixel's footprint, which a material cannot read); Brick's dirt and texture inputs are not translated. Numbers in `internal/redshift-nodes.md`, section 2, "Look-alikes".
- **Redshift nodes:** Color Correct's saturation above 1 is clamped to 1 by Blender (Redshift goes on; named in the console); a Color Correct setting that is wired counts with its value only (likewise a few settings of other nodes, each named); a Color Splitter's alpha output is not translated, and a colour wired into Color Composite or Color Layer counts as opaque (Blender's colours carry no alpha); a colour with alpha is taken as the Redshift of each Cinema 4D takes it (straight in 2027, premultiplied before), so a Redshift updated apart from Cinema 4D may differ [untested]. The projection nodes (TriPlanar, UV Projection, MatCap) project only a Texture node wired straight into them; anything else keeps its own coordinates (named). The Camera Map takes the frame's proportions from the Output settings, so the IPR's "Fit Window" shows it stretched. Round Corners and WireFrame work in Cycles only (Eevee has no Bevel; its Wireframe differs). The Ramp's noise, the State node's bump, the Scalar Ramp's tangents of points Redshift fills in itself, Ball and Cubic projections and a few modes are approximated or left out, each named (`internal/redshift-nodes.md`, section 2). Redshift's Vector Sqrt gives 0 in z, and so does the translation.
- **Cycles Native Material:** Cinema 4D's undo does not record an edit made in Blender; images are named by their full path, which works on one machine. Undo in Blender's editor is one history for all editor windows, and does not go back beyond the opening of the editor that was opened last.
- **Cycles nodes, Color Ramp:** at most eight stops, as plain settings (Cinema 4D's node descriptions have no gradient control); colour mode RGB only.
- **Cycles nodes, curves:** Cinema 4D's spline control shows and keeps only the square 0 to 1 (a spline of another range is rewritten into it on the first edit). For Vector Curves that square stands for Blender's -1 to 1 on both axes: the control's 0.5 is Blender's 0, and its labels read 0 to 1. Blender's points have no free tangents, so a curve is sent as up to a few hundred points (Blender's own table has 256 steps, which also rounds a corner by up to a quarter step times the change of slope, 0.003 for slopes of 2 and -1). Two knots at one position (a jump) become a rise over one 256th of the curve. A curve without knots gives 0, as Cinema 4D evaluates it, with a warning in the console. Selecting a knot in the control changes the setting's value (the selection is stored in it).
- **Cycles nodes:** what is wired into the output's Volume socket is not rendered. Combine Color and Separate Color have their Mode (RGB, HSV, HSL) since 0.2.0; it was left out before. Raycast's list of attributes (5.2, extendable sockets) is not there; its "Active Item Index" is a setting without effect.
- **Blender versions:** the comment "Needs Blender 5.3 or later" under a node newer than 5.0 is the node's own value: a comment the user writes replaces it (and the user can empty or hide it), but it cannot say whether the chosen Blender has the node (the card says that). The card on a node a version lacks is shown on every node newer than Blender 5.0, also when the chosen Blender has it (then grey, "Blender 5.3+"): whether a node shows a preview is fixed by its description and cannot follow the Preferences (measured, SDK reference). The card is drawn when Cinema 4D asks for the node's preview, so after another Blender is chosen it shows the new state only once Cinema 4D asks again. A socket or setting a version lacks is named in the console only when it is wired or not at its default. The material lighting nodes of 5.3 render black in Cycles (Blender's own behaviour; the console does not say so). A new node in Cinema 4D takes the newest version's defaults (Mix's Factor 1.0 and the Subsurface Scale 0.005 of 5.2 also with 5.0 and 5.1, since every value is sent), and the Render Settings 5.2's (4 defaults of 5.0 and 5.1 differ, listed in `tools/make_settings.py`).
- **Saving the IPR picture** writes RGB; alpha is not saved.
- **Cinema 4D versions:** each version is built with the last SDK of its year (2024.5, 2025.3.2, 2026.3, 2027.0); an earlier update of a year may refuse that build as newer than itself [untested], so users should keep Cinema 4D at the latest update of their year. In Cinema 4D 2024 the IPR's snapshot reaches the Picture Viewer without the name "Cycles Render IPR" (the 2024 SDK cannot name it), and Windows does not end a Blender the plugin started when Cinema 4D 2024 is killed while that Blender hangs (the 2024 SDK lacks the flag; Blender still ends when its connection closes). Before Cinema 4D 2026.2 the renderer cannot tell Cinema 4D that it evaluates the scene itself, so Cinema 4D prepares the render document for its own renderer first; the plugin undoes what was found ("Render Perfect" spheres), other effects of that preparation were not looked for. In Cinema 4D 2024 a Layer shader over a bitmap is baked again by every render (2024 gives its layers new identities in every render's copy of the document), and the IPR's "Add camera" does not keep the new camera in the render view once it is undone and redone (2024 drops the view's camera link on every undo and redo; the plugin then takes the view as the user's own choice). A scene saved with Cinema 4D 2027 and opened in 2026 renders in ACES2065-1 instead of ACEScg (Cinema 4D stores the render space as an index into its own OCIO configuration, and 2027's differs); Blender has no working space for ACES2065-1, the console says so, and the colours differ until the render space is set to ACEScg in the Project Settings.
- **Panoramic cameras are Cycles only.** Eevee renders a panoramic camera (the tag's, or Cinema 4D's spherical camera) as a perspective camera with the camera's focal length; the console says so. The IPR's free view (the fast path for a moving camera) is used for perspective cameras only; any other lens, depth of field or a film offset goes through a camera object.
- **Cinema 4D's spherical camera:** only the lat-long mapping is translated. A range without "Fit Frame" (which crops in Cinema 4D) is rendered as the whole sphere, the cubic mappings and the dome as lat-long; each is named in the console. Stereo is not read.
- **In the IPR's "Fit Window"** an equirectangular or central cylindrical panorama shows more of its range around the camera's picture; the other panorama types are stretched to the window instead [untested].
- **The fixed views and a parallel view without near clipping** see 10 km to either side of the camera's plane (Blender's camera stands 10 km back); a scene deeper than that is cut. An oblique view's camera has no motion blur of its own.
- **Cycles Camera tag:** the Attribute Manager shows its numbers with few decimals, so Blender's default polynomial coefficients (1e-5 and smaller) read 0; they are stored and sent exactly.
- **Thumbnails of Cycles node materials** are shown by Cinema 4D through the plain sRGB curve whatever the document's colour management: they agree with renders in a "Basic" document with Linear Workflow, and are brighter than renders in an OCIO document (no view transform: an Emission of 0.5 shows 188, a render 144) and in a Basic document without Linear Workflow (render 128). The preview Blender also loads textures in its own default way. A fix would have to find the material's document from the preview request, which carries none.
- **Colour:** render spaces other than ACEScg, linear Rec.709 and linear Rec.2020 are not supported; Cinema 4D's OCIO configuration is not handed to Blender (both use their own ACES definitions, which agree). With "Basic" colour management and Linear Workflow off, Cinema 4D puts the sRGB curve on linear images (EXR, HDR, a bitmap set to the Linear profile), which Blender cannot do when loading a file: a bitmap shader is then baked, and a file named by a node material or a Redshift dome light is used as it is, with a warning. Redshift lights and materials, other node materials and Cycles nodes are sent as in OCIO mode in a Basic document.
