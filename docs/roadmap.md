## What renders differently from 0.1.0

For the release notes of 0.2.0: a scene saved with 0.1.0 can render differently, on purpose.

- **Cinema 4D's lights cast shadows by default** (F20, the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-17). A light whose Shadow is "None" (Cinema 4D's default for a new light) cast no shadow in 0.1.0, as in the Standard renderer; now it casts one in Cycles and Eevee, like a light with any other shadow type. To have none, add a Cycles Light tag to the light, switch its Settings tab on and Cast Shadow off. Measured: `tests/c4d/light_shadows.py`; [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Shadows of Cinema 4D's lights".
- **Area and Shadow Maps (Soft) shadows are soft** (the F20 follow-up, the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-17). In 0.1.0 every shadow of an omni, spot or infinite light was sharp whatever its type; now an Area shadow is as soft as Cinema 4D's (the light gets a radius from its Details tab's area shape, an infinite light a sun angle from its Infinite Angle), and a Shadow Maps shadow gets a softness derived from its map size and sample radius (an approximation, matched at the light's Radius/Decay distance). The light's highlights on glossy surfaces grow with that size. Raytraced (Hard) and None stay sharp; an area light keeps its own size. [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Soft and area shadows".
- **A camera's near clipping is kept at 1 mm or more** (B10): a near clipping of 0 counts as none (the start of 1 cm Blender gets without clipping), and a start below 1 mm, or below a millionth of the far clipping (10 km without one), is raised to that. In 0.1.0 such a camera rendered no light in Eevee, and so did the editor camera of a scene without a camera in Cinema 4D 2024 and 2026 (with the 0.2.0 test release).
- **A light switched off by its green check in the Object Manager renders nothing** (B8). In 0.1.0 such a light (Cinema 4D's or Redshift's) still lit the scene, in final frames and in the IPR.
- **New render settings render on the GPU when there is one** (B11, the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-17). The Render Settings' Device has a third choice, "Automatic", which new settings start with: the GPU when Blender finds one (with the Preferences' device type and devices), else the CPU, which the console then names. In 0.1.0 new settings started with "CPU Compute" (Blender's default), and a GPU type chosen in the Preferences did nothing until the Render Settings said "GPU Compute"; a scene saved with either keeps it, so a scene of 0.1.0 still renders on the CPU unless it is switched (the IPR's status line and the console say so). Cycles' pictures on the GPU agree with the CPU's within noise.
- **A Redshift light follows its own Shadow switch** (with F20). In 0.1.0 every Redshift light cast shadows; one with Shadow off now casts none, as in Redshift.
- **Redshift lights are as bright and as coloured as in Redshift** (F24, asked for by the maintainer: "some area lights looked too dark", and the colour was off with the temperature mode). Measured against Redshift 2027.0.0 on a grey floor (`tests/c4d/redshift_lights.py`; the numbers are Cycles Render's brightness as a share of Redshift's, before and after). What changes in a scene of 0.1.0:
  - *An area light with "Spread" below 1 gets brighter:* under a 2 × 2 m light 2 m above the floor 0.69, 0.29 and 0.13 of Redshift for a spread of 0.8, 0.5 and 0.3, now 1.00, 1.03 and 0.93 (Redshift's spread concentrates the light, which 0.1.0 did not read; the edge of the lit patch is softer than Redshift's).
  - *An area light scaled as an object* now has the size the scale gives it, as in Redshift: scaled up it gets brighter (scale 2 in X: 0.34 to 0.66 before, now 0.97 to 1.00), scaled down darker (scale 0.5: 3.3 to 4.3 times too bright before).
  - *A light whose intensity unit is not "Image"* (lumen, candela, watt, luminance, radiance, lux) was read as if it were "Image" and rendered hundreds to thousands of times too bright (lumen 8600 times, watt 505, candela 683 on a point light); now as in Redshift.
  - *The colour temperature* ("Temperature" and "Temperature and Color") is now Redshift's colour in the document's render space; 0.1.0 used the light's colour (white by default). Such lights change colour and get darker: 3000 K is 0.64 times as bright as before (green and blue were 1.5 and 3.8 times Redshift's), 10000 K 0.80 times (red was 1.5 times), 6500 K slightly blue instead of white.
  - *Disc, sphere and cylinder shapes:* a disc whose two sizes differ is an ellipse now (it was a disc of the first size, 1.8 to 2.0 times too bright); a sphere is a sphere (it was a rectangle: 1.25 times under it, 0.3 to the side); a cylinder is a sphere as bright as the tube seen from its side (it was a flat rectangle, often lighting nothing).
  - *"Bi-Directional"* area lights shine to both sides (the back was dark), and *area lights are seen by the camera* as in Redshift (its ray contribution "Camera", on by default; they were invisible).
  - *Decay "Linear"* is matched halfway between Start and Stop (0.1.0 ignored it: with Redshift's default Stop of 1 m the light is now 0.125 times as strong); a point or spot light in an area light's unit (Redshift renders it black) is left out; a point or spot light in "Image" follows Redshift's render setting "Image Units Scale" (Redshift 2026 on; 100 by default, where nothing changes) when the scene's render data still holds Redshift's settings. Point, spot and infinite lights in "Image" and the plain rectangle and disc do not change (they matched to 1 % already).

## Open bugs

- [x] **Cinema 4D's interface lagged while the IPR followed with Cycles** (B11, reported from the 0.2.0 test release in Cinema 4D 2024; done in three steps 2026-11-17 and 18, not released; a hand at a real drag has not tried it yet). The cause of the report, found by the maintainer: the Render Settings said "CPU Compute" (their default) while the Preferences named CUDA or OptiX, which only says what "GPU Compute" uses; Cycles rendered on every core at the same priority class as Cinema 4D (which lowers itself to below normal), and the main thread was 90 to 97 % busy during a drag with waits up to 2 s (`internal/b11/measurements.md`). Now: new render settings start with the device "Automatic" (the GPU when there is one); the console says when the Render Settings' CPU makes a chosen GPU type do nothing; the IPR's status line names the device that really renders as Blender reports it, with the reason when it is the CPU; the IPR's warnings from the settings (a GPU that fell back to the CPU) reach the console (they were dropped in the IPR); a CPU render in the IPR leaves "CPU Threads Left Free" (Preferences; by default a quarter of the logical processors, at least 2) to Cinema 4D; the IPR's Blender runs one priority class below Cinema 4D's, never below "below normal" (idle stalled it entirely when Cinema 4D used every core); the bridge publishes and the window shows at most about 30 frames a second, the last one always, and a frame equal to the one on screen is not drawn again. Measured before and after: SDK reference, "Priority and cores of the IPR's Blender", "Frames the IPR shows". *Step 3, exporting less:* (a) a generator's mesh is judged by the hashes Cinema 4D keeps of its data instead of a pass over them: with a plane generator of a million quads in the scene (the "2.25 million" of the first measurements was this plane: it takes no more than 1000 segments a side) an export costs 1.5 to 2.5 ms instead of 12 ms (2024) and 21 ms (2027), and the 20 ms that Cinema 4D 2027's next redraw took after each such export are gone: the old hash asked the generator's UVW tag for its data, which 2027 then builds again and draws anew; (b) a redraw of a view exports only if the document's change counters moved, so navigating a view exports nothing where every redraw exported the scene before; (c) with the new preference "Optimized Live Updates" (on by default) a step of a drag sends only what moves: the camera, the lights, the place of moved objects with the objects below them, each item as the whole export writes it, and the whole scene once when the drag has ended; what cannot go alone (a deformer, the input of a generator, a Cloner, an instance, a volume) is exported whole in that step (since F23 the first four go alone too, as the meshes of the objects concerned: "Planned features", "A more responsive IPR"). Measured before and after, with that plane beside a dragged object, Cycles on the GPU at 960 × 540: a step of a tool's drag 43.8 → 3.5 ms in Cinema 4D 2027 (5.2 ms with the new switch off) and 16.2 → 3.6 ms in 2024, against 1.9 to 2.1 ms with the IPR closed; with Cinema 4D's events running the main thread 69 to 70 % busy → 6 to 7 % (2024: 23 % → 4.4 %); navigating a view 41 whole exports in 40 steps → none; on a stand-in of 8 logical processors 75 to 77 % busy → 9 to 10 % (`internal/b11/measurements.md`, "After step 3"; SDK reference, "What the IPR sends while a change is in progress"). Tested: `tests/c4d/ipr_optimized.py`, `tests/c4d/ipr_optimized_window.py`.

- [x] **The IPR entered from two threads: what a review found and closed** (2026-11-19, before 0.2.0, not released; none of it was seen to go wrong in use). Since 0.2.0 the scene hook has the IPR follow an evaluation on the thread that evaluates (a slider of the Attribute Manager). Closed: at Cinema 4D's shutdown the controller could be deleted while that thread was about to enter it (the pointer is atomic now, that thread is counted while it is inside, and the shutdown waits for it); that thread asked Cinema 4D for the view and its camera, which the SDK does not allow for on a thread other than the main thread (the main thread now hands it the camera as a link; a deleted camera, a view's own camera and a camera chosen since are left to the main thread); a call from the main thread's queue could enter the controller in the middle of an export on the same thread (it is not followed now); after a step that failed on that thread the main thread's steps went on until the next tick; a window that goes without being asked no longer leaves the hook following; the test getters read a step's figures under the lock. The contract is in `ipr/ipr_controller.h` (class comment) and in [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "What each thread may touch"; the test is `ipr_threads_window`. Left as it is: the main thread waits for a step that runs on the other thread (as long as a heavy mesh takes to export, 20 to 100 ms), which in the Attribute Manager's loop it would do anyway, since it has to stop that thread before it sets the next value.

- [x] **Small faults a review of the materials, tags and scene export found** (2026-11-19, before 0.2.0, not released; all as old as 0.1.0). Fixed: a hair material's gradient with three or more knots gave the tip the colour of the knot met last rather than the one at the end (Cinema 4D keeps knots in the order they were made); a quote or backslash typed into a Cycles node (an Attribute's or UV Map's name, a Principled Volume's attribute) made the whole scene request invalid, so nothing rendered; the Cycles Object tag's Temperature and Fire Grid Holds stayed editable with its Volume tab off. Named in the console now, where they were left out without a word: a texture tag's projection, offset and tiles on a material that is not classic; a texture the material exchange hands over for base weight, specular, IOR, coat IOR or emission strength; the hair material's settings besides thickness and colour; a node of a Cycles graph that is not a Cycles node (a Wire Rerouter), with its wires. Tested: `hair_particles`, `cycles_nodes` (section 10), `volumes`, `node_materials` (2024, 2026, 2027); the greyed settings by a screenshot (`tests/c4d/show_object_tag.py`); the exchange's line by reading only. Not a fault: the review thought a Redshift Texture node's alpha output was taken for its colour, but the node has no alpha output ([reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Redshift Standard Material").

- [x] **Undoing "Camera with Cycles Camera Tag" (the IPR's Add menu) left the render view without its camera** (found 2026-11-12 with F11, fixed 2026-11-13, not released). Cause, measured in `c4dpy` and in the application: Cinema 4D's undo cannot hold a view. After undoing any step that recorded the render view with `AddUndo` (`CHANGE`, `CHANGE_NOCHILDREN` or `CHANGE_SMALL`, the camera set with `SetSceneCamera` or as `BASEDRAW_DATA_CAMERA`), the document's view has no camera link at all and looks through its own editor camera (the "RS Camera", type 1057516, that is no object of the document); redo does not bring the link back; the view object held before the undo is no longer the document's (which is why the old check, reading that stale object, passed). Fix: the view stays out of the undo step; the plugin follows the step itself (`FollowAddedCameras` in `ipr/ipr_add.cpp`, called by the renderer's message plugin on every change event and once a second): while the added camera is gone the view looks through the camera it had before, once it is back (redo) through the added camera again; a switch is forgotten when its document closes or the user chooses another camera for that view. Tested: `ipr_add.py` (`camera_undone`, `camera_redone`, `camera_undone_again`; they fail on the old build), and with Cinema 4D's own Undo and Redo commands in the application (`ipr_window_checks.py`, `add_camera_undo_redo`). Side effect, by design: deleting the added camera by hand also puts the view back on the earlier camera (Cinema 4D itself would fall back to the editor camera).
- [x] **A scene of more than about 800 meshes could not be sent to Blender at all** (found 2026-11-13 with F16, fixed, not released). Every mesh is five blobs, the protocol allowed 4096 per frame, and every request carried an empty blob for each blob of an unchanged item it left out, so the first request and every later one of a 900-mesh scene (4500 blobs) made the bridge end the connection ("The connection to Blender was lost while sending"); final frames go through the same code. Now a request carries only the blobs of the items in it, and both sides accept 2^20 blobs (`protocol/protocol.md`, "Frames"). A bridge of 0.1.0 still refuses more than 4096. Tested: `test_protocol` (5000 blobs echoed in both modes), `ipr_reuse` (a 900-mesh scene in the IPR).
- [x] **A light switched off by its green check in the Object Manager stayed lit, in final frames and in the IPR** (B8, reported from the 0.2.0 test release in Cinema 4D 2024, fixed 2026-11-17, not released; in 0.1.0 too). Tested: `tests/c4d/light_enable.py` (against the Standard renderer and Redshift, the IPR through the test hook), `tests/c4d/ipr_lights_window.py` (the IPR window following the check by itself). The visibility dots worked before and still do.

- [ ] **The IPR can miss that the active document changed, when the document it showed was the only one and is closed.** Found while making the tests run several to one start of Cinema 4D (2026-11-12): `InsertBaseDocument` replaces an untouched "Untitled" document, so closing the inserted one leaves none and Cinema 4D makes a new empty one. In `c4dpy` after `tests/c4d/redshift_material.py` the IPR then never sent the new document (nothing sent in 8 s), kept the closed one's scene in Blender, and so kept its baked files; in a fresh `c4dpy` the same steps worked. - [x] **Colour management set to sRGB (legacy) gave wrong results** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). The render space "Legacy (sRGB linear workflow)" is Cinema 4D's "Basic" colour management. There, colours typed into Cinema 4D (materials, shaders, lights, the sky) rendered too bright with Linear Workflow on, because they were not linearised as Cinema 4D does; with Linear Workflow off every texture rendered too dark; with Multi-Pass on the picture got the sRGB curve twice; the IPR window applied the curve also without Linear Workflow; and the IPR's snapshot showed linear values without any curve in the Picture Viewer, too dark. The likely cause of the report: a texture without Linear Workflow, or a snapshot. Now every colour source, the buffer, the Picture Viewer, the IPR window, its snapshot and its saved EXR match the Standard renderer in every combination of Linear Workflow and input colour profile (`tests/c4d/colour.py`; numbers in the SDK reference, "Colour management"). What the dialog's legacy entry does to Linear Workflow and to the colours was not found out (its menu ignores posted input), so all combinations are covered. Not measured in that mode, and converted by analogy: clone colours, the hair gradient, the Cycles Object tag's volume colours, the world colour; see "Untested".
- [x] **Cinema 4D's fixed camera projections and the editor's parallel views rendered wrong** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). Every projection but Perspective and Parallel (Left, Right, Front, Back, Top, Bottom, Military, Frog, Bird, Gentleman, Isometric, Dimetric) was sent as a parallel camera turned like the camera object, while Cinema 4D ignores the camera's rotation for these and looks along the projection's own axes; the editor's views of those projections (no camera object) therefore showed a front view; the four oblique ones had no slant; a parallel camera clipped what lay behind its plane, which Cinema 4D shows; Cinema 4D's spherical camera rendered as a perspective one. All of it, final frames and the IPR, now matches the Standard renderer (`tests/c4d/cameras.py`, `camera_tag.py`; measurements in the SDK reference, "Cameras and views").
- [x] **Cycles nodes: the three curve nodes had no control for their curves** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). RGB Curves (C, R, G, B), Vector Curves (X, Y, Z) and Float Curve now have one Cinema 4D spline control per curve in the Attribute Manager, and the setting "Extend" (Blender's Extrapolated or Horizontal). A new node starts with a straight line and renders exactly as Blender's own new node. The plugin evaluates each spline as Cinema 4D draws it and sends it as points joined by straight lines; Blender's curve follows it within 0.0001 of its height (measured on S curves, hand-set tangents, cubic knots and overshooting Bezier segments), corners land exactly. Limits: see "Known limits", "Cycles nodes, curves". Tested by `tests/c4d/cycles_nodes.py` (a curve set as a spline renders as Cinema 4D evaluates it, for each node) and `tests/bridge/test_scene.py` (Cycles and Eevee).

## Known faults in translation

Each was measured and set aside. The test of each reports the number without failing.

- [ ] **Filter shader bakes too dark.** Hue is right, brightness is not (difference 0.31). Try Hue/Saturation, Brightness/Contrast and Gamma nodes instead of baking, or find the colour space the bake works in.
- [ ] **Shaders that work in 3D space are baked over the UV square.** The default Noise ("Texture" space), 3D gradients and the surface shaders show the right kind of pattern in the wrong place. Needs node versions of Cinema 4D's noises or a bake per object in its own space.
- [ ] **A bitmap's OCIO input colour space is not read.** Files are sent as sRGB, or linear for .exr and .hdr, whatever the shader says. Seen while fixing the legacy mode: an EXR written by Cinema 4D's `BaseBitmap.Save` without a colour profile renders in the Standard renderer as if it were sRGB (0.2 becomes 0.033), in Cycles Render as linear (0.2). EXRs of other programs were not tried. (With "Basic" colour management the bitmap's "Color Profile" is read.)
- [ ] **Cubic projection is mirrored or turned on some sides** (94.7 % agreement). It uses Blender's box projection of images; the fix is to build the six sides from nodes, and to apply the tag's offset there too.
- [ ] **Clone colours for MoGraph generators other than the Cloner.** Fracture, MoText, Matrix and the rest render without them. The Color shader's other modes and the Multi shader are not translated.
- [x] **Baked textures are removed, and reused** (fixed after 0.1.0, not released). Each Cinema 4D keeps them in a folder of its own, `<preferences>/cyclesrender/baked/session-<process id>-<time>`; final renders and the IPR reuse a file while nothing it depends on changed (an unchanged shader is not baked again at another frame unless it changes over time by itself); a file is deleted once every document it was made for has been closed and no Blender reads it, and everything goes when Cinema 4D quits. What a crashed session left, and what 0.1.0 left directly in `baked`, is removed at the next start. Left as it is: a document that was never in the document list (made by a script, rendered by the Render Queue or `Commandline.exe`) keeps its files until Cinema 4D quits. Tested by `baked_files` and `baked_files_window`.
- [ ] **Eevee does not blur a deforming mesh.** It ignores the `velocity` attribute; it needs the mesh itself to change over the shutter.
- [ ] **Instancers and lights have no motion blur.** The copies of a Cloner or multi-instance stand still during the shutter.
- [ ] **Pyro has never rendered real smoke in a test.** The code path is the one a Volume Builder uses, but a scripted simulation stays empty. Try a Pyro scene by hand, then check the density scale and the fire's temperature against Redshift.
- [ ] **Hair material:** only the colour gradient (its colours at root and tip) and the thickness are read; the console says so once for each Hair object with a hair material (since 0.2.0).
- [ ] **Redshift sheen is fainter** than in Redshift (difference 0.18).
- [ ] **Volumes made in Cinema 4D render empty in the Cycles of Blender 5.0 and 5.1**: Cinema 4D 2027 writes VDB format 225 (Volume Builder, Pyro), which their Cycles renders empty (Eevee shows it; files of format 224 of other programs render); the console says so. Use Blender 5.2 or newer for them. A way around would be writing format 224, if the SDK allows it [untried].
- [ ] **Redshift subsurface with Blender 5.0 and 5.1** differs more from Redshift (0.164 on 5.0.0, tolerance 0.15 on 5.2): Blender 5.2 changed how Random Walk maps the colour, and the older versions have only the old mapping. The test allows 0.2 before 5.2.
- [ ] **Redshift transmission depth, dispersion and thin-walled** are not translated; depth could become a Volume Absorption node.
- [ ] **OpenPBR's coat darkening** (on by default) darkens the base under a coat in Redshift, which Blender's coat does not: a dark blue coated ball renders 0.14 brighter (`redshift_nodes`, `openpbr_coat`, reported without failing; named in the console).
- [ ] **Redshift's AO node** samples a cone (spread, falloff) that Blender's Ambient Occlusion node lacks: a corner renders 0.075 darker; spread and falloff are named in the console.

## Known limits

- **The IPR needs a desktop session** and renders into a hidden Blender window. Its resolution can exceed the desktop's (3840 × 2160 tested), but every frame it publishes is read back on Blender's main thread: about 1 ms at 960 × 540 and 20 ms at 3840 × 2160 (since 0.2.0 at most 30 a second).
- **The IPR shows no motion blur** (Cycles' viewport has none), and it uses Blender's viewport settings, so small differences from the final render are possible.
- **On the CPU a 1920 × 1080 IPR is slow to react** (0.8 s for an edit). Use the GPU or a smaller IPR resolution. In the IPR a CPU render leaves "CPU Threads Left Free" to Cinema 4D (a quarter of the logical processors by default), so it is that much slower than a final render.
- **The IPR shows at most about 30 pictures a second** (0.2.0, B11), and Blender publishes no more: enough to follow a drag, and what kept Cinema 4D's interface free. The last picture is always shown.
- **The IPR during a drag:** an object or camera moved with a tool is sent to Blender at every step of the drag, but whether the window can draw the new pictures before the drag ends depends on Cinema 4D's drag loop and was not seen (no test can make a real drag). While a value is dragged in the Attribute Manager, what can be sent alone follows: a light's values, a camera's values, an object's place in the Coordinates tab, and since F23 the values of primitives, generators and deformers (with "Optimized Live Updates"; without it only the lights); any other value dragged there (a material, a tag's value, the sky) and the lights a generator makes follow when the mouse is released. Navigating a view (Alt and the mouse) is followed when the drag ends, unless Cinema 4D lets the IPR window's timer run or sends a message during it (unknown); navigating in the IPR's own picture is followed during the drag (since 0.2.0). During a tool's drag the picture can also be one step behind the drag (the tool's message comes before the step is evaluated, and the update after the evaluation waits when Blender is still busy with the step before; measured in emulated drags of a deformer: the last step's mesh came with the export at the end of the drag in 4 of 24; [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "A tool's drag makes two calls a step").
- **"Optimized Live Updates": what can differ during a drag** (0.2.0, B11). A step of a drag sends only what moved and what the document's change counters show to depend on it; the whole scene is exported when the drag ends (and the picture is then exactly a whole export's). In between, something that follows a moved object without any counter of Cinema 4D moving stays where it was: known kinds are a shader that reads an object's place and a plugin's generator that reads another object without declaring it. Since F23 a step that builds a cache anew (a generator's parameter, its input, a deformer, a moved Cloner or instance) collects the objects concerned again and sends their meshes alone; what a step still exports whole (a tool's drag) or leaves to the end (a drag in the Attribute Manager): an object that shows a volume, hair, particles, the sky, a dome light or a light made by a generator; two generators that share a mesh because they make the same one (the copies of generators that make 16 or more, the clones of two Cloners); an object whose items come or go while a light has an include or exclude list; a tag's value, a material, points edited by hand, a render dot, a renamed object. A light's include and exclude lists are those of the last whole export. **A heavy mesh follows at the pace of its export:** a step that changes it takes as long as the export (a Bend on 250,000 quads 16 to 27 ms, a plane of a million quads 70 to 100 ms, on the thread that evaluates the document: the main thread for a tool, Cinema 4D's drawing thread for a slider), and after such a step the next ones are left out for three times its length, also for a small object dragged at the same time; the picture then shows the newest step that was sent until the next one or the release. After a redraw without a change event, a change that moves no counter (a node material's value set by a script; in 2024 a polygon selection or the document's unit set by a script) reaches Blender a quarter of a second later, with the settle export; made by hand such changes send a change event and are exported at once. With "Live Updates During Drags and Playback" off (Preferences), nothing is sent during a drag or playback: a view's navigation that lets the IPR window's timer run (unknown, see above) would still be followed at the timer's pace, as in 0.1.0. With "Skip Unchanged Meshes" the walk, objects and materials are still exported after every change: a scene of tens of thousands of objects or many complex materials still costs time per change; hair, volumes and particles are always exported in full.
- **Navigating from the IPR's picture:** the orbit's and dolly's rates per pixel (0.4° and e^-0.005 at 100 % speed) and the dolly's direction (right and up bring the camera nearer) are estimates, to be compared with a view by hand (Cinema 4D's own rates could not be measured: posted input does not drive a view); all are constants at the top of `ipr/ipr_navigation.cpp`. The wheel zooms the picture and does not dolly. The point under the mouse is found on meshes only (not hair, volumes, particles) and not through a panoramic lens (a panorama orbits its point of interest and pans by an approximate scale). What Cinema 4D's view keeps as its point of interest is not readable: the IPR uses the camera's target distance, or where its own last drag left the point. Cinema 4D's Ctrl "view snapping", "Deep" and "Sync Orthographic Views" are not followed. A camera driven by animation stays where the drag left it until the time changes, as in Cinema 4D's view (no keys are set). Cinema 4D's status bar says what is moved, or why nothing is; its managers (the Attribute Manager's coordinates) update when the drag ends.
- **Redshift lights** (since 0.2.0, F24; [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Redshift lights"): what Blender has not, each named in the console. *Spread* is another profile in Blender: the plugin fits Blender's spread and the light's power to Redshift's light on a floor, but Blender's lit patch has a soft edge that lies farther out (under a 2 m light 2 m above the floor, the share of Redshift's brightness at the patches Redshift lights: 0.98 to 1.00 for spread 0.8, 0.95 to 1.33 for 0.5 with 1.03 below the light, 0.93 to 0.97 for 0.3; Redshift's own edge is sharp, so beyond it Blender's light goes on where Redshift's has ended). *Decay "None" and "Linear"* are matched at one distance (None at 1 m, Linear halfway between Start and Stop); closer the light is brighter, farther darker than in Redshift. A *cylinder* is a sphere as bright as the tube from its side, too bright along its axis (about 1.5 times beyond its end). The *spot's edge* fades linearly in the angle in Redshift and by a smoothstep in Blender (the cone and the light inside it agree; in the fade 0.7 to 1.1), and its "Falloff Curve" is not translated. Not translated: a mesh as the area light's shape (a rectangle of its size), a texture on the light, IES profiles, the Physical Sun's sky (a plain infinite light), portals. The back side of a bi-directional light is a second light in Blender and is not part of a Cycles Light tag's Light tab. Spheres and cylinders are not seen by the camera (Redshift shows a sphere with twice its radiance, as a shell seen through). *An older Redshift* (3.6.04, Cinema 4D 2024's) counts point and spot lights, "Normalize Intensity", every unit but "Image", spread and an area light's decay otherwise (by its unit scale and photometric settings: with its default of 1 cm a point light is about 10000 times darker than in Redshift 2026 and 2027): such lights are translated as Redshift 2026 and 2027 render them, with a line in the console; area and infinite lights in "Image" agree. Redshift 2026 and later's render setting "Image Units Scale" is followed. *Eevee* ignores spread (such a light is as wide as a plain one: 0.54 of Redshift under a light of spread 0.5), and leaves a light out beyond sqrt(power / 100 / Light Threshold) metres, which Redshift's physical intensities reach quickly: with the Render Settings' default Light Threshold of 0.01 a 2 × 2 m area light of intensity 1 (12.6 W in Blender) ends 3.5 m away and a point light of 2000 lumens 1.7 m away; lower the Light Threshold (1e-4 reaches ten times as far).
- **Lights without falloff** cannot be reproduced by a physically based renderer; they match Cinema 4D at one distance. Cinema 4D's area lights shine to both sides, Blender's to one.
- **Shadows of Cinema 4D's lights** depart from the Standard renderer on purpose (since 0.2.0, F20): every light casts them, also with Shadow "None"; only a Cycles Light tag (Settings tab, Cast Shadow) turns them off. Their softness comes from the light's size in Blender: an Area shadow's edge matches Cinema 4D's within a few per cent at any distance (a rectangle's edge, which in Cinema 4D depends on its side across the edge, is taken as a round light of the same area; shapes other than rectangle, disc and sphere are approximated, with a note in the console); a Shadow Maps shadow's blur, which in Cinema 4D does not depend on where the caster stands, is matched only at the light's Radius/Decay distance with the caster halfway (closer to the floor Blender's edge is sharper, farther from it softer); its bias is not read. Eevee draws these soft edges narrower than Cycles (about 0.7 to 0.95 of the width). A light given a size shows larger, softer highlights on glossy surfaces than in the Standard renderer.
- **Camera clipping:** a near clipping below 1 mm, or below a millionth of the far clipping (10 km without one), is raised to that, because Eevee renders no light with a wider range (B10); geometry within that distance of the camera is clipped also in Cycles.
- **Reflectance** is reduced to one specular lobe and one clear coat; the Principled BSDF has no layer stack.
- **Displacement** moves only the points a mesh has (no sub-polygon displacement).
- **A Sphere primitive with "Render Perfect"** shows its polygons; Cinema 4D's own renderers draw it perfectly round.
- **Clones that all differ** become one instancer each, which is slow from a few thousand on.
- **Two texture tags on one object that use different UVW tags:** the mesh has one render UV set, that of the tag covering the whole object.
- **A texture tag's projection, offset and tiles** count for classic materials only; a node material, a Redshift material or any other is placed as with UVW mapping without offset or tiles. Since 0.2.0 the console names such a material when its tag says otherwise.
- **Render passes** are written as rendered; the view transform is never baked into them. With "Basic" colour management that is what Cinema 4D's own layers hold too. Eevee has no UV, indirect, transmission, volume indirect, object or material index, sample count or shadow catcher pass; Cycles has no Shadow and no Transparent pass (measured with Blender 5.2.3; the Render Settings hide each for the engine that lacks it, and a pass asked for anyway is named in the console). The Vector pass is Cycles's and Eevee's motion to the frame before and after, in pixels; Cycles gives none with motion blur on. The Object Index is the first enabled Object Buffer of a Compositing tag (further ones are named in the console); the Material Index is the "Pass Index" of a Cycles node material's Material Output (classic materials: 0). Cinema 4D's own Object Buffer layers (one mask per id) are not made.
- **Pixel aspect:** the IPR renders square pixels and shows the picture as wide as the pixel aspect makes it (400 × 200 pixels of aspect 2 are 800 × 200 in the IPR at "100 %"). The Output tab's edits are not part of Cinema 4D's undo for the Output page (undo restores the tab's own values, which then show Cinema 4D's again), and Cinema 4D's Film Aspect field is not updated by them (it was not when Python set the pixel aspect either; Cinema 4D's picture follows Width, Height and Pixel Aspect).
- **Image nodes:** a path made relative is relative to the folder of the active document (the buttons work on the material the Node Editor shows); an image sequence is not translated (its file is used as one image, with a note in the console); a file of the Asset Browser is copied into the session's folder of texture files [untested].
- **Render settings without effect:** the four Subdivisions values act on Blender's adaptive subdivision, which the plugin does not use (it sends the mesh Cinema 4D subdivided).
- **Cycles Light object:** it does not light Cinema 4D's viewport or Cinema 4D's own renderers. Its light node tree and light groups are not there. Its viewport drawing shows type, size and cone at a fixed display size; it has no handles.
- **Cycles Light tag:** Blender's radius, soft falloff, sun angle and spread come only with the Light tab, which replaces all of the light's emission; with the tab off the light renders as before. A light casts shadows (Cinema 4D's light whatever its "Shadow" type, a Redshift light by its own Shadow switch) until the Settings tab's Cast Shadow decides. The tag counts on the light it sits on only.
- **Render devices:** the device list is what a Blender reported when "Find Devices" was pressed (a new card needs another press); the material thumbnails still render on the CPU. Several GPUs of one type were not tried.
- **Redshift's look-alikes** (Jitter, Random Color Switch, Brick, Flakes): each object gets another random value than in Redshift (the same ranges and shares, varying with the same objects: per path of names, per Redshift Object ID, per User Data number; one value for the copies of a render instance or multi-instance, as in Redshift); Brick's and Flakes' noises and cells are Blender's (Brick's layout, gap widths, colours and spreads, Flakes' size, share and tilt measured to agree); Flakes do not fade with distance (Redshift fades by the pixel's footprint, which a material cannot read); Brick's dirt and texture inputs are not translated. Numbers in `internal/redshift-nodes.md`, section 2, "Look-alikes".
- **Redshift nodes:** Color Correct's saturation above 1 is clamped to 1 by Blender (Redshift goes on; named in the console); a Color Correct setting that is wired counts with its value only (likewise a few settings of other nodes, each named); a Color Splitter's alpha output is not translated, and a colour wired into Color Composite or Color Layer counts as opaque (Blender's colours carry no alpha); the Texture node has no alpha output of its own (its one output is `outcolor`: [reference/c4d-sdk.md](reference/c4d-sdk.md), "Redshift Standard Material"); a colour with alpha is taken as the Redshift of each Cinema 4D takes it (straight in 2027, premultiplied before), so a Redshift updated apart from Cinema 4D may differ [untested]. The projection nodes (TriPlanar, UV Projection, MatCap) project only a Texture node wired straight into them; anything else keeps its own coordinates (named). The Camera Map takes the frame's proportions from the Output settings, so the IPR's "Fit Window" shows it stretched. Round Corners and WireFrame work in Cycles only (Eevee has no Bevel; its Wireframe differs). The Ramp's noise, the State node's bump, the Scalar Ramp's tangents of points Redshift fills in itself, Ball and Cubic projections and a few modes are approximated or left out, each named (`internal/redshift-nodes.md`, section 2). Redshift's Vector Sqrt gives 0 in z, and so does the translation.
- **Cycles Native Material:** Cinema 4D's undo does not record an edit made in Blender; images are named by their full path, which works on one machine. Undo in Blender's editor is one history for all editor windows, and does not go back beyond the opening of the editor that was opened last.
- **Cycles nodes, Color Ramp:** at most eight stops, as plain settings (Cinema 4D's node descriptions have no gradient control); colour mode RGB only.
- **Cycles nodes, curves:** Cinema 4D's spline control shows and keeps only the square 0 to 1 (a spline of another range is rewritten into it on the first edit). For Vector Curves that square stands for Blender's -1 to 1 on both axes: the control's 0.5 is Blender's 0, and its labels read 0 to 1. Blender's points have no free tangents, so a curve is sent as up to a few hundred points (Blender's own table has 256 steps, which also rounds a corner by up to a quarter step times the change of slope, 0.003 for slopes of 2 and -1). Two knots at one position (a jump) become a rise over one 256th of the curve. A curve without knots gives 0, as Cinema 4D evaluates it, with a warning in the console. Selecting a knot in the control changes the setting's value (the selection is stored in it).
- **Cycles nodes:** what is wired into the output's Volume socket is not rendered. A node of a Cycles graph that is not a Cycles node (Cinema 4D's Wire Rerouter; a group or another node of Cinema 4D's own by reading, untested) is left out with its wires, so what it is wired into takes its own value; since 0.2.0 one that is wired is named in the console (a scaffold, which has no wires, is not). Combine Color and Separate Color have their Mode (RGB, HSV, HSL) since 0.2.0; it was left out before. Raycast's list of attributes (5.2, extendable sockets) is not there; its "Active Item Index" is a setting without effect.
- **Blender versions:** the comment "Needs Blender 5.3 or later" under a node newer than 5.0 is the node's own value: a comment the user writes replaces it (and the user can empty or hide it), but it cannot say whether the chosen Blender has the node (the card says that). The card on a node a version lacks is shown on every node newer than Blender 5.0, also when the chosen Blender has it (then grey, "Blender 5.3+"): whether a node shows a preview is fixed by its description and cannot follow the Preferences (measured, SDK reference). The card is drawn when Cinema 4D asks for the node's preview, so after another Blender is chosen it shows the new state only once Cinema 4D asks again. A socket or setting a version lacks is named in the console only when it is wired or not at its default. The material lighting nodes of 5.3 render black in Cycles (Blender's own behaviour; the console does not say so). A new node in Cinema 4D takes the newest version's defaults (Mix's Factor 1.0 and the Subsurface Scale 0.005 of 5.2 also with 5.0 and 5.1, since every value is sent), and the Render Settings 5.2's (4 defaults of 5.0 and 5.1 differ, listed in `tools/make_settings.py`).
- **Saving the IPR picture** writes RGB; alpha is not saved.
- **Cinema 4D versions:** each version is built with the last SDK of its year (2024.5, 2025.3.2, 2026.3, 2027.0); an earlier update of a year may refuse that build as newer than itself [untested], so users should keep Cinema 4D at the latest update of their year. In Cinema 4D 2024 the IPR's snapshot reaches the Picture Viewer without the name "Cycles Render IPR" (the 2024 SDK cannot name it), and Windows does not end a Blender the plugin started when Cinema 4D 2024 is killed while that Blender hangs (the 2024 SDK lacks the flag; Blender still ends when its connection closes). Before Cinema 4D 2026.2 the renderer cannot tell Cinema 4D that it evaluates the scene itself, so Cinema 4D prepares the render document for its own renderer first; the plugin undoes what was found ("Render Perfect" spheres), other effects of that preparation were not looked for. In Cinema 4D 2024 a Layer shader over a bitmap is baked again by every render (2024 gives its layers new identities in every render's copy of the document), and the IPR's "Add camera" does not keep the new camera in the render view once it is undone and redone (2024 drops the view's camera link on every undo and redo; the plugin then takes the view as the user's own choice). A scene saved with Cinema 4D 2027 and opened in 2026 renders in ACES2065-1 instead of ACEScg (Cinema 4D stores the render space as an index into its own OCIO configuration, and 2027's differs); Blender has no working space for ACES2065-1, the console says so, and the colours differ until the render space is set to ACEScg in the Project Settings.
- **Panoramic cameras are Cycles only.** Eevee renders a panoramic camera (the tag's, or Cinema 4D's spherical camera) as a perspective camera with the camera's focal length; the console says so. The IPR's free view (the fast path for a moving camera) is used for perspective cameras only; any other lens, depth of field or a film offset goes through a camera object.
- **Cinema 4D's spherical camera:** only the lat-long mapping is translated. A range without "Fit Frame" (which crops in Cinema 4D) is rendered as the whole sphere, the cubic mappings and the dome as lat-long; each is named in the console. Stereo is not read.
- **In the IPR's "Fit Window"** an equirectangular or central cylindrical panorama shows more of its range around the camera's picture; the other panorama types are stretched to the window instead [untested].
- **The fixed views and a parallel view without near clipping** see 10 km to either side of the camera's plane (Blender's camera stands 10 km back); a scene deeper than that is cut. An oblique view's camera has no motion blur of its own.
- **Cycles Camera tag:** the Attribute Manager shows its numbers with few decimals, so Blender's default polynomial coefficients (1e-5 and smaller) read 0; they are stored and sent exactly.
- **Thumbnails of Cycles node materials** are shown by Cinema 4D through the plain sRGB curve whatever the document's colour management: they agree with renders in a "Basic" document with Linear Workflow, and are brighter than renders in an OCIO document (no view transform: an Emission of 0.5 shows 188, a render 144) and in a Basic document without Linear Workflow (render 128). The preview Blender also loads textures in its own default way. A fix would have to find the material's document from the preview request, which carries none.
- **Colour:** render spaces other than ACEScg, linear Rec.709 and linear Rec.2020 are not supported; Cinema 4D's OCIO configuration is not handed to Blender (both use their own ACES definitions, which agree). With "Basic" colour management and Linear Workflow off, Cinema 4D puts the sRGB curve on linear images (EXR, HDR, a bitmap set to the Linear profile), which Blender cannot do when loading a file: a bitmap shader is then baked, and a file named by a node material or a Redshift dome light is used as it is, with a warning. Redshift lights and materials, other node materials and Cycles nodes are sent as in OCIO mode in a Basic document.
